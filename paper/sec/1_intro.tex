\section{Introduction}
Spiking neural networks (SNNs) promise event-driven, multiplication-free inference, and the last three years have produced spiking transformers trained from scratch~\cite{zhou2023spikformer,yao2023spikedriven}, a fully spiking CLIP~\cite{li2023spikeclip} and conversion pipelines that turn a whole pretrained transformer into an SNN~\cite{you2024spikeziptf,jiang2024sta}. A practitioner with an existing vision-language model, however, rarely wants to replace it; they want to know \emph{where} a spiking block can be inserted or substituted cheaply, \emph{whether} the inserted block has learned, and \emph{how} to set its surrogate slope, time budget and threshold. These questions are usually answered by running the downstream benchmark after every change.

HTSR theory~\cite{martin2021htsr,martin2021predicting} offers a benchmark-free alternative: the empirical spectral density of $W^\top W$ of a well-trained layer has a power-law tail with exponent $\alpha$ near 2, under-trained layers have $\alpha \gg 2$, and $\alpha$ has been used to allocate learning rates~\cite{zhou2023tempbalance} and pruning ratios~\cite{lu2024alphapruning} per layer. In a companion study on small spiking MLPs we found that a healthy spiking layer reaches the same $\alpha$ as its dense twin, that $\alpha$ rises with the surrogate slope and falls with the fraction of silent units, and that lookup tables live in a different $\alpha$ regime from dense layers. This paper tests whether these observations survive the move to a pretrained multimodal model, and whether they can be turned into a certificate (H1), a conversion-order rule (H2) and a design guide for a spiking lookup memory (H3).

\paragraph{Contributions.}
(1) A per-role $\alpha$ profile of a LAION-400M CLIP ViT-B/32. (2) A single-block ANN-to-SNN conversion study on 24 transformer blocks with ReLU and clipped-ReLU ablations, showing which part of the damage a weight-spectrum statistic can predict. (3) Fine-tuning trajectories of spiking replace-activation and adapter inserts with $\alpha$, firing fraction and five downstream metrics measured jointly, including surrogate-slope and time-budget sweeps and dense controls. (4) Cumulative conversion in six orders. (5) A spiking L3 lookup memory~\cite{tseng2026l3} as a CLIP adapter against dense MLP and dense L3 adapters. (6) Two conversion fixes specific to CLIP-style MLPs (per-channel thresholds and bias correction) without which none of the above is measurable.
